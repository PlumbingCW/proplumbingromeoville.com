Main Sewer Line Clog Repair Cost in Romeoville, IL

The cost to clear a clogged main sewer line depends on where the blockage sits, what tool reaches it and whether the pipe is damaged. A Romeoville quote is a sum of those parts, not a flat rate.

This guide walks through each part so you can read a quote line by line. We leave dollar figures out on purpose, because the same symptom can be a short cable visit or a dig.

By the Romeoville Pro Plumbing team · Published October 29, 2026

Four things set the price of a main sewer line clog repair in Romeoville. They are the distance from the cleanout to the blockage, the tool needed to break it, how easy the line is to enter, and whether the pipe is cracked or collapsed. Clearing paper and grease near a cleanout is a short visit. Cutting roots forty feet out takes a bigger machine. Repairing the pipe is a separate job with its own price.

Cleaning and repair are two different prices

Most quotes mix up two jobs, and it helps to pull them apart. Cleaning removes whatever is blocking the pipe. Repair fixes the pipe so the blockage does not form again. A homeowner with a clog caused by too much paper needs only the first. A homeowner with roots pushing through a cracked joint needs both, usually in that order.

That is why a good plumber asks questions before naming a number. A camera run after the cleaning shows which job you are really buying. Our sewer line cleaning visits stop at the clear pipe. If footage shows damage, the repair is quoted separately and you decide.

What happens before the quote

  1. You describe which fixtures are slow, which drains gurgle and whether water has surfaced in the basement.
  2. The plumber finds the cleanout, usually a capped pipe in the basement, garage or yard.
  3. A cable goes in to find the blockage and measure how far away it sits.
  4. If the clog returns within weeks or the cable hits a hard stop, a camera goes in to see why.

Each step answers a question that changes the price. The cable tells you the distance. The camera tells you the cause. Skipping the camera on a repeat clog means paying for the same visit twice.

Cable, jetter or camera: the line items you may see

A cable machine spins a steel cable through the blockage. It is the usual starting point for a first-time clog. A jetter pushes water through the pipe at high pressure and scours grease and scale off the wall. It costs more per visit and fits lines that clog on a regular basis. A camera is a diagnostic add-on that often pays for itself by ruling out a repair.

Ask for the quote to name the tool

A written or spoken quote should say which tool is being used and whether a camera look is included. If it only says main line clear, ask what happens when the cable hits a root mass or a broken joint. The answer shows how the plumber handles surprises.

When cleaning is not enough

A clean pipe that clogs again is telling you something. Roots enter through joints in older clay tile. Cast iron scales and flakes. Plastic pipe can sag where the trench settled. In each case the next step is a sewer line repair, and the price depends on how much pipe is affected and how the plumber reaches it.

Signs the problem is the pipe, not the clog

  • The same drain backs up again within a few weeks of being cleared
  • Footage shows roots, a crack or a section that holds standing water
  • Several lower-level fixtures gurgle when the washer drains
  • A damp patch or sunken strip of lawn sits along the line to the street

Repair options run from a spot repair of one short section to lining a longer run to replacing the line. The shorter the damaged section and the easier it is to reach, the lower the price. A line under a driveway or a mature tree costs more to reach than one under open lawn.

Romeoville sewer work: licensing and permit notes

Illinois licenses plumbers through the Illinois Department of Public Health under the Illinois Plumbing License Law. The state says anyone who installs, alters, repairs or maintains plumbing must hold a license. Check the state licensing information before you sign anything.

Permit rules differ by town. Joliet lists underground plumbing, sewer and water services, rough-in and final among the inspections its building division performs. Crest Hill states that most residential and commercial projects need a permit. Bolingbrook says permits are required for repairs and asks for notice before an inspection.

Romeoville has its own rules, so ask the Village of Romeoville building department whether your repair needs a permit before anyone digs. If it does, expect that step to add time to the schedule.

How to compare two sewer quotes

  • Check that both quotes cover the same job. One may be a clearing and the other a clearing plus a camera.
  • Ask what is charged if the first tool fails and a second is needed.
  • Ask whether footage is saved so you can keep a copy.
  • Confirm whether permits and restoration are included for any digging.
  • Ask when the price is stated, before work starts or after the first hour.

A low number that leaves out the camera or the permit is not a lower price. It is a smaller scope. The comparison only works when the scope matches.

Insurance and sewer backups

A standard homeowner policy often treats a worn-out pipe as maintenance, not a covered loss. Damage from a backup, such as ruined flooring, may be covered only with a separate water backup endorsement. Read your policy or call your agent before the plumber leaves, and take dated photos of any water damage.
