What Is Leak Detection? A Romeoville, IL Homeowner Guide

Leak detection is the practice of locating a hidden water leak before anyone cuts into drywall, concrete or lawn. It uses sound, pressure, heat and cameras to point at the exact spot.

By the Romeoville Pro Plumbing team · Published October 29, 2026

Leak detection is how a plumber finds water escaping from a pipe you cannot see. Instead of guessing and opening walls, the technician narrows the search with listening gear, pressure tests, thermal imaging or a camera, then marks the spot. You pay to open one small area rather than three.

Most people call after one of four clues: a water bill that jumped, a meter that keeps moving when everything is off, a warm patch on the floor, or a stain on a ceiling. Any of them is worth a short test before you spend a dollar.

Run this meter test first

  1. Turn off every tap, the dishwasher, the washing machine and the ice maker. Check that no toilet is running.
  2. Open the meter cover and look at the small leak indicator, usually a triangle or a tiny wheel. Note whether it moves.
  3. If you have a reading dial, write down the number and wait thirty minutes without using water. Check it again.
  4. If the number changed or the indicator keeps turning, water is leaving somewhere. Close the main shutoff and watch the meter again.
  5. If the meter stops with the main closed, the leak is inside your house. If it keeps running, the leak is between the meter and the shutoff.

That last step saves time. It tells the technician whether to start inside the walls or out in the yard under the service line.

Where hidden leaks tend to hide

The usual suspects are supply lines under a slab, joints behind a shower wall, a pinhole in a copper run, a water heater connection, and the service line that crosses the yard. Each calls for a slightly different tool. Under a slab, sound and pressure do most of the work. Behind a tile wall, thermal imaging and a moisture meter narrow things down quickly.

Irrigation lines and outdoor spigots leak too, and they are easy to overlook. A soggy corner of lawn that never dries between rains is worth a look.

Signs of a hidden leak

  • A water bill that rises with no change in how your household uses water
  • A meter that moves with every fixture off
  • The sound of running water in a quiet house
  • A warm or damp spot on a floor, or a patch of lawn that stays green and soggy
  • Peeling paint, bubbling wallpaper or a musty smell in one room
  • Low pressure at several fixtures at once

Romeoville note: where your side of the meter starts

The Village of Romeoville's drinking water quality report explains that the shutoff valve before the water meter is owned and maintained by the resident. For you, the meter is the dividing line for the test above: a leak after the meter is your repair, and the valve in front of it is your responsibility to keep working.

The same report lists its 2024 tap results for copper at 0.145 ppm against an action level of 1.3 ppm, and it names corrosion of household plumbing as a likely source of lead and copper. Corroded interior pipe is also a common site of pinhole leaks, so a copper-heavy house with a rising bill deserves a closer look.

The Village also publishes a service line material inventory on its water quality reports page. If you want to know what runs between the street and your meter, start there, then call Public Works with questions about the Village's side of the line.

After the leak is located

Once the spot is marked, the repair can be small. A single joint or a short section of pipe is often a quick fix. If the problem is the buried line, water service line repair may mean spot repair or a replacement section.
