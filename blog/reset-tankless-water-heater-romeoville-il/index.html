How to Reset a Tankless Water Heater in Romeoville, IL

A tankless heater that shows an error code or runs cold has usually locked itself out for a reason. A reset clears the lockout, but it only helps if the cause was a one-time fault.

Below is a safe reset routine, a way to read the common causes, and the point where the unit needs a descale or a technician.

By the Romeoville Pro Plumbing team · Published October 29, 2026

To reset a tankless water heater, turn off the power at the unit or its breaker, wait about a minute, then turn it back on. This clears a lockout caused by a brief fault such as a power dip, a flow sensor hiccup or a flame that went out. If the same code returns, the cause is real and needs fixing, not resetting.

A reset routine in order

Smell gas? Leave first

If you smell gas near the heater, do not touch any switch. Leave the house, then call the gas utility and emergency services from outside. A reset is only for a unit that is not leaking gas.

  1. Read the error code on the display, or note the pattern of the indicator lights, and write it down.
  2. Turn off the hot water at all fixtures and close any open hot taps.
  3. Switch off power to the heater using its switch or the breaker, and leave it off for about a minute.
  4. Check that the gas valve to the heater is fully open, and for electric units that the breaker is on.
  5. Restore power and wait for the unit to run through its start-up checks.
  6. Open a hot tap fully and let it run for a couple of minutes to see if the unit holds temperature.

Write the code down before step three. Many units clear the code when power is cut, and the code is the best clue a technician can have. Your manual lists what each code means for your brand.

When scale is the culprit

Hard water leaves mineral scale inside the heat exchanger. The scale narrows the water passage and insulates the metal, so the unit works harder to deliver the same temperature. Eventually it trips a limit and locks out. A reset helps for a day or two, then the code returns.

The fix is a descale flush. A pump circulates a mild acid, often white vinegar or a maker-approved solution, through the unit for a set time, then clears it with fresh water. Follow your manual, since procedures and intervals differ by brand. Our tankless water heater service includes flushing and scale checks.

Signs your tankless unit needs a descale

  • Hot water takes longer to arrive than it used to
  • Output temperature drifts down during a long shower
  • A flow or temperature error code returns after each reset
  • Rumbling or kettling noise from inside the unit
  • White or tan flakes in the aerator of a hot faucet

Checks you can do before calling

  • Clean the inlet water filter screen if your unit has one
  • Make sure the vent and intake are clear of snow, nests or debris
  • Look for a kinked or closed shutoff on the cold supply
  • Check the condensate drain on high-efficiency models for a blockage
  • Confirm that the thermostat or app setting has not been changed

Do not remove the front cover or touch gas components. Those checks belong to a licensed technician. If the cause is not on the list above, a water heater repair visit finds it faster than repeated resets.

How to keep a tankless heater from locking out

Schedule a descale at the interval the maker recommends, and sooner if you see the signs above. Keep the area around the unit clear and the vent free of obstruction. Run the unit through a normal cycle after a long absence, such as a vacation, to confirm it starts without help.

Install a water softener if your tap water is hard. It does not replace flushing, but it cuts how fast scale builds. Ask your water utility for its annual report or test your tap to see how hard your supply is.
