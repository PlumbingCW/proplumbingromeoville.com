Cleaning Out Storm Drains: A Romeoville, IL Homeowner Guide

Before you pick up a rake or a hose, work out whose drain it is. Yard drains and downspout lines on your lot are yours to clear. The grate in the street belongs to the Village, and the right move there is to report it.

By the Romeoville Pro Plumbing team · Published October 29, 2026

Most storm drain trouble in a residential neighborhood comes down to leaves, grass clippings, mulch and silt in a drain opening. Cleaning that debris from a yard drain is a ten-minute job. Cleaning anything in the public street is not yours to do, and it is not safe to try.

Four things people call a storm drain

Which drain is which, and who handles it
What it isWhere you find itWho clears it
Street grate or catch basinIn the curb or road edgeThe Village. Report it, do not open it
Yard or area drainA grate in a low spot of your lawn or patioYou, or a plumber if it is buried deep
Downspout lineA pipe that carries roof water from the gutter undergroundYou, or a plumber if it is clogged underground
Window well drainAt the bottom of a basement window wellYou, with a plumber for the pipe below
Basement floor drainA grate in the basement floorThis is usually the sewer, not the storm system. See the note below

That last row matters. A basement floor drain is normally connected to the sanitary side of your plumbing. If it bubbles during a storm, the cause may be a sewer backup rather than a storm drain, and you want a plumber on the line, not a garden rake.

What you can safely do yourself

  1. Wait until the rain stops and the standing water is gone. Do not step into pooled water over a drain.
  2. Wear gloves and lift leaves, sticks and trash off the grate by hand or with a rake.
  3. Scoop silt out of the basin with a trowel if the grate on your own lot lifts out easily.
  4. Flush the pipe with a garden hose and watch whether the water leaves quickly or backs up.
  5. Check each downspout extension and splash block to confirm it carries water at least several feet from the foundation.
  6. Put the grate back in place so no one trips on an open hole.

Leave the street grate alone

Do not lift a street grate or climb into any public drain, manhole or catch basin. They are heavy, dangerous and part of the Village's system. If one is blocked or flooding, report it to the Village instead.

Why a yard drain keeps backing up

If a drain on your lot fills again after you clean the grate, the blockage is underground. Roots find the joints in buried pipe and grow into them. Silt settles at low points. Crushed or offset pipe holds water at the bend. A hose will not reach any of it.

A cable machine can break through a clog, and a camera shows what the pipe looks like. For heavy silt and root hair, hydro jetting scours the pipe wall with high-pressure water. It suits the large-diameter lines that carry roof and yard runoff because it clears the whole circumference, not just a hole down the middle.

Romeoville note: what the Village asks of residents

The Village of Romeoville's Storm Sewer Program page describes how the system works. Rain that falls on yards runs to the street and into the storm sewer. The Village asks residents to rake leaves and sweep up grass clippings before rain can wash them to the street, and to sweep driveways and walks instead of hosing debris into the gutter. That keeps the grate in front of your house clear.

The Village also publishes a stormwater drainage concern form on its Stormwater Reports page, and its annual stormwater report says Public Works takes calls from property owners about stormwater matters. Use those channels for a blocked street inlet, a flooded road edge or a suspected illegal discharge. The report also notes that the Village's street sweeping program cuts down on catch basin and sewer cleaning, which is another reason to keep clippings out of the road.

Village ordinances prohibit discharging polluted water into natural outlets. Do not rinse paint, oil or pool chemicals into a drain, public or private.

A seasonal checklist

Storm drain upkeep by season
SeasonWhat to checkWhy
SpringYard drains, downspout extensions, window well drainsThaw and heavy rain test everything at once
Early summerSilt and mulch around lawn gratesFresh mulch washes into low spots fast
FallLeaves on every grate and gutterLeaf drop is the top cause of street and yard blockages
Late fallDisconnect hoses, confirm extensions point away from the foundationFrozen, clogged lines cannot take snowmelt

Standing water after a storm is not always a blockage. Lockport's city drainage page notes that stormwater may take up to 72 hours to clear after heavy rain, so give a flat lot time before you assume the pipe is stopped. If water is still sitting well beyond that, or it enters a window well, act sooner.

When to call a plumber

  • A yard or downspout drain backs up again within days of cleaning
  • Water pools against the foundation or fills a window well
  • A basement floor drain gurgles or rises during rain
  • You see roots, a collapsed section or a sinkhole along a buried pipe
  • You are unsure whether a line is storm or sewer

Our drain cleaning crews work on private yard and downspout lines as well as indoor drains. We do not touch Village storm sewers, inlets or manholes.
