Toilet Keeps Clogging? What Romeoville, IL Homes Need to Know

A toilet that clogs once is a nuisance. A toilet that clogs again and again has a cause, and the cause is usually one of three things: the toilet itself, the drain behind it, or what goes down it.

Work through the checks below in order. They separate a bad toilet from a partly blocked line, which are fixed in very different ways.

By the Romeoville Pro Plumbing team · Published October 29, 2026

When a toilet keeps clogging, ask one question first: is it only this toilet, or are other drains slow too? A single toilet that clogs with normal use points to the bowl, the trap or the way it flushes. Several fixtures that struggle together point to the drain line behind them. That one answer saves you from guessing at the fix.

Checks for a single stubborn toilet

Watch the flush

Flush with the lid off the tank and watch. The bowl should empty with a strong swirl, then refill. A weak flush that leaves water high in the bowl suggests blocked rim holes or a partly closed fill valve. Mineral scale clogs the small holes under the rim and the siphon jet at the bottom of the bowl.

Check the water level in the tank

If the tank water sits too low, the flush has less force. Look for a fill line marked inside the tank. Raise the float if the level is under that mark. A level that rises past the overflow tube is a fill valve fault and a different repair.

Look for something stuck in the trap

Children's toys, a cotton pad or a cleaning brush head can lodge in the curved passage at the bottom of the bowl. A closet auger, not a drain snake, is the tool made for this. It has a protective sleeve that keeps the porcelain from scratching.

Use the right plunger

A flange plunger has a rubber flap that fits the toilet outlet. A flat cup plunger made for sinks does not seal as well. Press slowly to push water, then pull back sharply. Several slow strokes work better than a few hard ones.

If the closet auger finds nothing and the flush looks strong, the toilet itself may be the culprit. Older low-capacity bowls can have a weak siphon, and the fix is a toilet repair or replacement with a model that suits your drain.

When the drain line is the real cause

A toilet drains into the same line as the tub, the sink and often the washer. If that line has roots, a buildup of paper or a sag, every fixture suffers, but the toilet shows it first because it moves the most water at once. The tell is a gurgle in one fixture when another drains.

Signs the drain line is restricted

  • A bubbling sound from the toilet when the washer runs
  • Water backing up in the tub after a flush
  • Two or more bathrooms slowing at once
  • A clog that returns within days of being cleared
  • Sewage smell near a basement floor drain

In that case no plunger will help for long. A cable or a jetter has to open the line, and a camera look shows whether roots, a crack or a low spot caused it. Our drain cleaning crew starts with the cleanout and works toward the toilet.

What should and should not go down

  • Toilet paper and human waste only
  • No wipes, including those sold as flushable
  • No cotton balls, swabs, dental floss or hair
  • No paper towels, which do not break down like toilet paper
  • No feminine products or diapers

Wipes are the most common reason a line clogs again and again. They do not break down, and they wrap around roots and rough joints until they form a plug. If the household cannot stop using them, keep a bin by the toilet.

When the blockage is not yours: municipal sewers near Romeoville

Not every backup starts on your property. Plainfield's public works department says it maintains over 200 miles of sewer main and 16 wastewater pumping stations, and it asks residents to call it about sewer problems, with an after-hours line for urgent ones. Lockport's Public Works sewer division handles collection of sewage and maintenance of the collection system.

The tie-back is a quick test. If your neighbors on the same street have backups too, or the cleanout in your yard is clear and full of water, the blockage may be in the public main. Call the municipal utility that serves your home.

When to stop DIY and call

Stop if water rises when you flush, if a plunger and an auger fail to clear the bowl, or if sewage appears at a floor drain. Further effort can push the blockage deeper or spill wastewater into the house. The same applies to any toilet that clogs more than twice in a month.
