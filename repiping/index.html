Whole House Repiping in Romeoville, IL

Rusty water, weak pressure and pinhole leaks often mean the supply pipes have worn out, not that one more patch will do. We plan and carry out whole house repiping in Romeoville, IL, and our 24/7 line takes your questions at any hour.

Call (630) 822-9290All services

Repiping replaces the supply lines that carry water from the main to every faucet, shower and appliance. It does not touch the drains. If your Romeoville home has galvanized steel, polybutylene or aging copper, replacement may cost less over time than repeated leak repairs and the wall damage that comes with them.

When repiping starts to make sense

Signs your supply pipes may be failing

  • Brown or orange water when a faucet has not run for a few hours
  • Weak flow at several fixtures, not just one
  • Repeated pinhole leaks or green crust on copper joints
  • Pipes that clang, shudder or whine
  • Water that tastes of metal
  • Damp spots on drywall with no obvious source

Replacing galvanized pipe

Galvanized steel was a standard supply pipe in many homes built before the 1960s. It is steel with a zinc coating that wears away, so the inside rusts from the surface outward. The pipe shrinks until water barely trickles, and the rust flakes can foul faucet screens and appliance valves.

You can often spot it at the water meter or at an exposed run in the basement: gray metal with threaded joints, and sometimes a rust-colored bloom at the fittings. If you see that, ask for an inspection before the next leak decides the timing for you.

What age tells you about Romeoville houses

Hampton Park, for example, was developed in 1957, and a house of that era could have galvanized steel, copper or a mix after earlier repairs. Subdivisions built later may have copper, and homes from the 1980s and early 1990s are the group most likely to contain polybutylene. The only way to know is to look at the pipe, which we do on the visit.

How a repipe is carried out

  1. We inspect the existing lines, test pressure and plan the new route with you.
  2. The water is shut off and the old supply lines are drained and disconnected.
  3. New lines go in, usually PEX or copper, through attic, basement or crawl space whenever possible to limit wall openings.
  4. Every fixture and appliance is reconnected, and the system is pressure-tested for leaks.
  5. Small access holes are patched or left for a drywall finisher, as agreed in advance.

Water is off for part of each day while we work, so plan for showers and dishes accordingly. We plan the schedule with you in advance. A larger home or an old one with surprises behind the walls may need more days.

PEX or copper

PEX is flexible plastic tubing that bends around corners, so fewer joints and smaller openings are needed. It also resists freezing damage better than rigid metal. Copper lasts for decades and is preferred by some owners for its long record. Either can be correct, and local water chemistry and your budget decide.
