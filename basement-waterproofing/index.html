Basement Waterproofing in Romeoville, IL

Water in a basement has a cause, and the fix depends on where the water enters. We trace the source, then build an interior drainage path that carries it to a sump basin and out of the house. Dispatch answers 24/7 if water is rising.

Basement waterproofing in Romeoville, IL starts with finding out how the water gets in. A damp patch on one wall, a puddle at the floor edge and a film of moisture across the slab are three different problems. Treating them all with the same coat of sealer is why so many basements stay wet.

Where the water comes from

Water reaches a basement in four main ways. It seeps through porous block or poured concrete. It pushes up through the joint where the floor meets the wall. It rises through cracks in the slab when the ground below is saturated. Or it comes from a leaking pipe that only looks like groundwater.

We test for that last one first. A plumbing leak is a pipe repair and not a waterproofing job, and a quick check of the meter and the supply lines saves money on the wrong fix.

How interior drainage works

We cut a narrow trench along the inside of the footing and lay perforated drain tile in clean stone. The tile catches water at the point where it enters and carries it to a sump basin. A pump then lifts the water out and discharges it away from the house.

The floor is patched with new concrete over the trench. The system works with the water instead of trying to hold back groundwater from the outside, which would require digging up the yard and the foundation.

  • Perforated drain tile set in washed stone along the footing
  • A sealed sump basin with a lid, set below the slab
  • A pump with a check valve and a discharge line routed away from the foundation
  • An optional battery backup for storms that knock out power
  • A wall-side barrier or channel where block walls weep

What waterproofing does not do

Drain tile and a pump manage water. They do not fix a bowed wall, a settling foundation or a large structural crack. If we see signs of movement, we say so and suggest you call a structural engineer or foundation specialist before spending money on drainage.

Paint-on sealers can hide a damp wall for a season, then peel off when pressure builds behind them. We do not use a coating as the only fix for active seepage.

Signs the basement needs a drainage plan

  • Musty smell that gets worse after rain
  • White mineral deposits on walls or the floor edge
  • Peeling paint or bubbling wall finish
  • Stored boxes that feel damp or grow mold
  • Water at the floor edge that returns each wet season
  • A sump pump that runs for hours but the basement still gets wet

Why Romeoville basements stay damp

Romeoville sits in low, flat country close to the Des Plaines River and the Illinois and Michigan Canal. In ground like this, water has little slope to run off and soaks down instead. After a thaw or a string of wet weeks, the water table can press on foundations and slabs.

In a finished basement, block walls take in more moisture than poured concrete. A wall that looks fine can still wick water into carpet or drywall. Ask us to check behind finished walls if a musty smell lingers.

Gutters, grading and downspouts

Part of the solution is outside the house. Downspouts should empty several feet from the wall. The soil near the foundation should slope away. Fixing these costs little and cuts the water the drain tile has to move.
