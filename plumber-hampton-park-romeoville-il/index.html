Plumber in Hampton Park, Romeoville, IL

Hampton Park is a named subdivision area of Romeoville, Illinois, and Village records treat it as a group of numbered plats. This guide pulls together what the Village has published about water, meters and permits, then adds plain plumbing advice you can use at home.

Where a fact comes from a Village document, the sources are listed at the bottom of the page. Anything the Village has not published is marked as something to ask Public Works.

Where Hampton Park sits in Romeoville

The Village Resident Packet says the original Hampton Park land was farm ground on the west bank of the Des Plaines River, along what is now Route 53. A second section was annexed later to join the contiguous village. Later zoning ordinances list Hampton Park Subdivision Nos. 1 through 13 as a group, so the name covers many plats rather than one block.

Because the name spans so many plats, two neighbors can have different lot lines, drains and utility easements. When a plumber asks which subdivision number you live in, your plat or closing papers will answer it better than the street name.

What homeowners here commonly ask a plumber

These are general questions that come up in any older or newer suburb, not claims about this neighborhood in particular. The table below shows what you can check yourself and when the problem has outgrown a DIY fix.

A single slow fixture is often a local clog. When the whole house drains slowly, the blockage may sit in the main line, and drain cleaning on one branch will not fix it. A sewer line cleaning visit clears the main and lets a camera show what caused it.

What the Village has published that matters at home

Remote meters. The Resident Packet says the Village is installing SENSUS water meters that are read remotely and track hourly usage. If your bill jumps, ask Water Billing for your hourly usage. A flat overnight line above zero points to a running toilet or a leak, and leak detection can find where.

Spring flushing. Hydrants are flushed each spring. The Packet says discolored water can follow and is not harmful. Run the cold tap first, and hold off on the washer if the water is brown so laundry is not stained. Public Works answers questions at 815-886-1870.

Watermain work. The Packet describes an ongoing watermain replacement plan aimed at pipes prone to breaks and leaks. Ask Public Works whether work is planned on your street, since a shutoff can disturb sediment and change pressure for a day.

Groundwater now, Lake Michigan later. The Village runs on groundwater wells today. Its 2025 Water Quality Report reports no lead mains or lead service lines. Lake Michigan water through the Grand Prairie Water Commission is expected around 2030, which is a plan only. If you are buying a softener or heater, ask what the Village expects to change before spending.

Check the main shutoff before you need it

Walk to the main valve where the line enters the house and turn it a quarter turn each way once a year. A valve that is stuck on the day a pipe splits is a much bigger problem. If it will not move, a plumber can replace it on a calm day.

Sump pumps are worth a test if your home has one. Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the float lift and the pump shut off. Check that the discharge line runs outside and away from the foundation. Sump pump repair is a routine job when a float sticks or a pump runs without ever shutting off.

Water heaters follow the same pattern in every town. A tank that rumbles, leaks at the base or runs out quickly is worth a look before it fails. Water heater repair often means a new element, thermostat or anode rod, and the tank label shows the install date.

Permits, codes and calling 811

Illinois follows the Illinois Plumbing Code. The Village asks you to check with its Building Division before home improvement work, at 1050 W Romeo Rd or 815-886-7200. Its permit page does not name plumbing, so confirm by phone before replacing a water heater, moving a drain or adding a bathroom.

Irrigation is different. The Resident Packet says sprinkler systems need a permit and that backflow devices must be inspected every year. If you plan to dig for a new line or a sprinkler, call 811 first so utilities are marked. The Village also limits lawn watering to odd and even days in summer, so check current hours with Public Works.

Hampton Park plumbing questions

The Village flushes hydrants every spring to clear sediment from the mains. The Resident Packet says discolored water can follow and is not harmful to drink, though it can stain laundry. Run a cold tap for a few minutes. If it stays cloudy, call Public Works before assuming your pipes are at fault.

The Village's 2025 Water Quality Report does not report lead mains or lead service lines. That covers the Village side. Fixtures and pipes inside a house are separate, so a home can still be tested if you want to be certain.

The Village says it expects Lake Michigan water through the Grand Prairie Water Commission around 2030. That is a plan, not today's supply. Homes now run on Village groundwater wells. Ask Public Works what changes in hardness or treatment when the switch is scheduled.

The Village asks residents to check with the Building Division before starting home improvement work. Its permit page does not single out plumbing, so call to confirm. Irrigation systems do need a permit, and their backflow devices must be inspected every year.

The Village water service area reaches into parts of unincorporated Plainfield and Bolingbrook, so an address alone does not prove Village limits. Your water bill shows your provider. For billing questions, the Village lists a water billing office in the Resident Packet.
