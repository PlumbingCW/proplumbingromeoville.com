A tankless water heater heats water only when you open a tap, so there is no tank to leak or run out. In Romeoville, IL we install them, repair them and flush them. Some shops only swap them out when they act up. We service them first.

Flushing and descaling a tankless heater

Flushing is the single most useful thing you can do for a tankless unit. Water passes through a narrow heat exchanger. Minerals in that water bake onto the metal as scale, which restricts flow and forces the unit to work harder. Eventually it throws an error code, delivers lukewarm water or shuts down.

We handle the job in a set order. First we shut off gas or power and the water supply. Then we hook up a pump, hoses and a descaling solution to the service valves on the unit. The solution circulates through the heat exchanger and dissolves the scale. Finally we rinse the system, clean the inlet screen and test the unit.

Service valves are essential. If your heater does not have them, we can add a set so future flushes are quick. We also check the vent, the condensate drain on high-efficiency models and the display for stored fault codes.

Signs a tankless heater needs descaling

  • Hot water that runs lukewarm or fluctuates between hot and cold
  • Error codes tied to flow, temperature or the heat exchanger
  • A noticeable drop in flow at the shower
  • Rumbling or kettle-like noise from the unit
  • Hot water that takes longer to arrive than it used to

How often depends on your water. Ask the Village of Romeoville for its water quality report, or test your tap with a hardness strip. Harder water means more frequent flushing. Your manufacturer's manual sets a minimum interval, and many recommend yearly service in hard-water areas. A softener ahead of the heater cuts the scale at its source.

Tankless units are rated by how many gallons per minute they can heat at a given temperature rise. Winter tap water is colder than summer, so a unit that keeps up in July may struggle in January. We size for the coldest water your supply delivers.

Installing a tankless heater in an existing home

Switching from a tank usually means new venting, since tankless gas units run hot and use sealed stainless or approved plastic vent pipe. The gas line may need upsizing because the burner demands a large burst of fuel. Electric models need heavy circuits, and some homes need a panel upgrade.

We assess all three before quoting. Our installation team handles venting and gas sizing. If an old tank is leaking right now, replacement gets hot water back faster, and a tankless upgrade can follow when you have time to plan.
