Emergency Hydro Jetting in Romeoville, IL

Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub a pipe clean from the inside. We offer it in Romeoville 24/7 when a line keeps backing up and a cable has not held.

A licensed plumber looks at the line first, then decides whether jetting is the right fix or a gentler method will do.

Hydro jetting in Romeoville, IL is available 24/7 for lines that back up into basements, floor drains and tubs. A cable pokes a hole through a clog. A jetter washes the whole pipe wall, so the line flows like new instead of like a straw. If your drains are backing up right now, stop running water and call so a jetter can be lined up.

If water is backing up through a floor drain right now

Jetting is not always the first tool for an active backup. Often we open the line with a cable to get water moving, then jet it once the flood risk is gone. That order keeps pressurized water from being forced against a blockage that has nowhere to go.

  • Stop laundry, showers and dishwasher cycles at once.
  • Keep people and pets away from standing water in the basement.
  • Do not pour bleach or drain chemicals into the line.
  • Note which drains are affected and send dispatch a quick description.

Grease, roots and scale: what jetting removes

Grease is the most common target. It hardens on the pipe wall into a white or tan layer that thickens each year. Jetting heats and shears it off, then flushes it downstream where it cannot catch the next load of waste.

Fine tree roots behave differently. They enter at a joint and grow into a hairy mat. A rotating nozzle cuts that mat back, though the root will regrow if the joint stays open. That is why a repeat visit often ends with a sewer line repair quote.

Scale comes from the water itself. Northern Illinois water is often hard, so ask the Village of Romeoville for its water quality report or test your tap, because minerals harden on pipe walls over the years. A jetter removes a good share of it, and a water softener slows the buildup afterward.

When we advise against jetting

Jetting a broken pipe can make things worse. A cracked or sagging line may spray water into the soil, and the pressure can pull loose pieces out of position. If a line looks damaged, we run a camera first. Older clay tile or cast iron is more likely to need that step, which matters in subdivisions from the 1950s such as Hampton Park.

Signs a jetting visit makes sense

  • The same drain clogs every few weeks after cabling
  • Slow drains in several fixtures at once
  • Gurgling in toilets when the washer runs
  • A greasy smell or visible white buildup at a cleanout
  • Roots found by a previous camera inspection

What happens during a jetting visit

  1. We find or install access at a cleanout, or pull a toilet if none exists.
  2. A camera or cable pass shows what is in the line and whether the pipe is sound.
  3. The jetter nozzle goes in and works from the far end back toward you.
  4. We run water through to confirm it flows freely.
  5. You get a plain explanation of what we found and how to prevent a repeat.

Codes and permits

Jetting a line does not change the pipe, so it usually needs no permit. If the camera finds a failed section that must be replaced, that repair falls under the Illinois Plumbing Code and the Village of Romeoville building department sets the permit and inspection steps.

Before we arrive

  • Move pets and children away from open drains
  • Do not run water or flush once the crew is on site
  • Tell us about any past root or grease problems
