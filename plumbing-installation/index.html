Gas safety first: if you smell gas, leave

Leave first, then call

Rotten-egg or sulfur smell, a hissing sound near a gas line or appliance? Leave the building now and take everyone, including pets, with you. Do not flip light switches, use a lighter or start a car in the garage.

From outside, call 911 and your gas utility's emergency number, which is printed on your gas bill. Call us afterward for the repair. We are a plumber, not the gas utility, and we never promise a response time for a suspected leak.

Most installation work in Romeoville falls into three groups: adding or replacing fixtures, building new plumbing for a bathroom or laundry, and connecting gas appliances. Each begins with measuring what already exists, because the cost depends more on pipe routes than on the fixture you buy.

Basement bathroom plumbing

A basement bathroom has one big question: where does the wastewater go? Drains in a concrete floor slab sit lower than the house's main sewer line in many homes. If so, the toilet and sink need a sewage ejector pump with a sealed basin instead of a gravity drain.

We open the slab only where needed, set drain and vent lines, rough in supply lines and test them before the concrete is patched. Tell your building department about the project early, since permit rules vary and the Village of Romeoville can say which inspections apply.

Gas line work: what we do and what we leave alone

A gas fitting is not a place for guesswork. We run and test gas lines for appliances like ranges, dryers, fireplaces and water heaters, and we check every joint with a pressure test and leak solution before the line goes into service. We do not work on the utility's meter or street-side piping.

Converting an appliance between natural gas and propane is controlled by the manufacturer, who supplies a conversion kit and instructions. A stove or fireplace insert must be approved for the fuel it will burn. Call before you buy so we confirm what your model allows.

Gas warning signs, stop and leave

  • A rotten-egg smell in the kitchen, basement or near the water heater
  • A hissing or whistling sound from a pipe or appliance
  • Dead or discolored grass above a buried gas line
  • A pilot flame that burns yellow or orange rather than blue
  • Dizziness, headache or nausea indoors, which can also signal carbon monoxide

Planning around an older house

Homes from the late 1950s, like those in Hampton Park, were built before today's fixtures and appliances. The supply pipe may be small, the drain may be cast iron or clay and there may be no shutoff near the spot you want a new sink. We check these things during planning so the new fixture has water pressure and a drain that works.

If the existing supply is rusted, repiping before you build can be cheaper than opening finished walls later. A new bath or kitchen also raises demand on your water heater, so we compare its size to the added load.

How an installation job runs

  1. Walkthrough to see the location, pipes, drains and any obstacles.
  2. Written scope and price, agreed before work begins.
  3. Rough-in of supply, drain and vent lines, plus pressure testing.
  4. Inspection if your village requires one, then fixture setting.
  5. Final test of every connection and a walkthrough with you.
