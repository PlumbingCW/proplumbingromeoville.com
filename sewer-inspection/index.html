Sewer Camera Inspection in Romeoville, IL

A sewer camera inspection in Romeoville, IL shows what is inside your main line instead of guessing. We feed a waterproof camera down the pipe and record the whole run for you.

Book a planned visit during the week, or call the 24/7 line if the pipe is already backing up.

Call (630) 822-9290All services

Most sewer lines are buried out of sight until they fail. A camera inspection turns that blind spot into a video you can watch. It is the fastest way to learn whether a backup comes from grease, roots, a crack or a sag, and it is a smart check before you buy an older home in Romeoville.

What the camera actually shows

The camera head sits on a flexible rod with lights and a distance counter. It travels from a cleanout or a pulled toilet toward the street. As it moves, we note every joint, bend, sag and defect, and record the footage with the distance from the entry point.

What appears on screenWhat it usually meansTypical next step

Reasons to schedule an inspection

Good times to book a camera look

  • Backups that return after each cleaning
  • Several drains slow or gurgling together
  • A sewer smell with no clear source
  • Buying a home built before the 1980s
  • Planning a basement bathroom or remodel
  • A sinkhole in the lawn above the line

Before you buy a home

A standard home inspector does not usually run a camera through the lateral. A sewer scope can reveal a cracked line before closing, when you still have room to negotiate. Ask the seller for cleanout access and give us a window of a few hours. We give you the video and a plain summary.

Older areas such as Hampton Park, a subdivision developed in 1957, are exactly where a scope is worth the small cost. Pipes of that age may be clay or cast iron, and both can crack or flake with time.

How the visit works

  1. We locate a cleanout, or remove a toilet when none exists.
  2. The line is cleared enough for a clear picture. Heavy buildup may need a separate cleaning.
  3. The camera travels to the street connection while a locator tracks its depth and position above ground.
  4. We mark any defects on the ground if you want to see where they sit.
  5. You receive the recording and a plain-language explanation of what it means.

Backup in progress?

A camera works best on a flowing line. If water is rising now, call the emergency number and we will stop the backup first.

Locating the line and its depth

A locator picks up the camera transmitter from above, so we can mark the line on the lawn or driveway. That helps if a repair is needed. It also tells you how deep the pipe runs and whether it passes under a driveway, a patio or a tree.

Codes and permits

A camera inspection is a look, not a repair. Any repair that follows falls under the Illinois Plumbing Code, and the Village of Romeoville building department decides what needs a permit.
