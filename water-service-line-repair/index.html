Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Romeoville, IL

The water service line is the buried pipe that carries city water from the street to your house. When it leaks, splits or clogs with scale, you notice it at every faucet. We repair and replace these lines in Romeoville day or night.

Our emergency water service line repair in Romeoville, IL is available 24/7. A failing line can lose hundreds of gallons a day without a single drip inside the house, so the first sign is often a bill or a soggy lawn. While you wait for us, shut the main valve inside the house, note the meter reading and keep people and pets off any soft ground.

If water is surfacing in the yard right now

Wet ground along the path from the street to the house, a sunken strip of lawn or a hissing sound near the foundation all point to a line that has opened up. Close the main shutoff and watch the meter dial. If it still moves with every fixture off, the leak sits between the meter and the house, and that stretch belongs to the homeowner.

  1. Shut off the main valve where the line enters the house, usually near the water heater or in a basement corner.
  2. Look at the meter or its small leak indicator. Write down the number and the time.
  3. Check again in twenty minutes. Any movement means water is escaping somewhere in the buried run.
  4. Do not dig around the spot yourself. Gas, electric and cable lines often share the same trench.
  5. Call dispatch and describe what you saw. Mention if the water is clear or muddy.

Finding a leak you cannot see

A buried leak rarely shows where the water comes up. Water follows sand and gravel along the pipe and surfaces far from the break. We confirm the leak with a pressure test on the isolated line, then use listening gear and a line tracer to pinpoint the spot before any trench is opened.

That saves digging a long ditch for a break that sits in one place. Where the problem could just as well be inside the walls or under the slab, our leak detection service narrows it down first.

Why the water coming from the street can feel weak

Low pressure has several causes, and the service line is only one of them. Start with a simple test. If pressure is weak at every tap and the outdoor spigot, the problem is upstream of your fixtures. If only one faucet is weak, look at that fixture's aerator or supply valve instead.

Older steel service lines clog from the inside as mineral scale and rust narrow the opening. A partly closed main shutoff or meter valve gives the same symptom. So does a failed pressure reducing valve, if your house has one. We check each in turn before recommending a new line.

Signs the service line is the problem

  • Water bill jumps with no change in how you use water
  • Meter dial turns when every fixture is off
  • Weak flow at every faucet, including outdoors
  • Soft, green or sunken patches of lawn on the line path
  • Muddy or discolored water after the line has been worked on
  • Hissing or running sound near the meter or foundation

Replacing a line: trench or trenchless

A full replacement can be done by open trench or by pulling a new pipe through a narrow bore. Trenching is simpler and lets us inspect the whole route. Boring costs more in equipment but saves a driveway, a walkway or a mature tree.

Which one fits depends on what sits on top of the line. We walk the route with you before choosing, and we say plainly if one method is not practical on your lot.

What Romeoville ground does to buried pipe

Northern Illinois soil freezes deep in winter and thaws unevenly in spring. A service line must sit below the frost line, and shallow or shifted pipe is more likely to crack during a hard freeze. If your line failed in late winter, ask us to check its depth when we expose it.

Many Romeoville homes also sit close to the Des Plaines River and the Illinois and Michigan Canal, where groundwater can run higher than on elevated land. Wet soil around a pipe speeds corrosion on steel and hides small leaks for a long time.

New homes, additions and added fixtures

Builders and owners adding a bathroom, a garage sink or a second kitchen sometimes ask whether the existing line can carry the extra load. We size the line to the fixtures it must serve. A new build or a large addition may call for a larger pipe from the meter, which is easier to install before the lawn and plantings go in.

Codes and permits

Water service work follows the Illinois Plumbing Code, and the Village of Romeoville decides who handles the section of line at the meter. Ask the Village where its responsibility ends before work starts.
