A burst pipe repair call in Romeoville, IL is answered 24/7, including holidays and the coldest nights of the year. Before anything else, stop the water at the main valve. Every minute the pipe runs adds to the damage, and our crew can work on a dry pipe far faster than a live one.

If a pipe has burst right now: shut off the water

  1. Find the main shutoff, usually in the basement near the meter or where the line enters the house, and turn it clockwise until it stops.
  2. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines and relieve pressure.
  3. Turn off the water heater's power or gas so it does not run dry.
  4. Shut off electricity to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  5. Move furniture and valuables away from the water and take photos for your insurer.

Frozen pipes: thaw safely or wait for us

Never use open flame

Do not use a torch, kerosene heater or propane device on a frozen pipe. Fire and hot spots can ignite framing or crack the pipe. A hair dryer on low or warm towels is safer.

Northern Illinois winters freeze pipes in exterior walls, garages, crawlspaces and unheated utility rooms. If a faucet trickles or gives nothing in a cold snap, a section is probably frozen. Open the faucet, warm the pipe slowly, and watch for leaks as the ice melts. A frozen pipe may already be cracked, and it shows only when thawed.

If you see frost on the pipe, a bulge or any wet drywall, shut the main water off first. Then call before thawing. Thawing a split pipe sends water straight into the wall.

Why pipes crack or split

  • Ice expansion inside a freezing pipe, the cause behind most winter bursts
  • Corrosion thinning old galvanized or copper walls from the inside
  • Pressure spikes from a stuck regulator or water hammer
  • Loose fittings and joints that finally give way
  • Physical damage from a nail, a remodel or settling

Cracked pipe repair: what we do

Once the water is off, we cut out the damaged section and replace it. In copper we sweat a new length with soldered fittings, and in PEX we use crimp or expansion fittings. A short clamp can hold a pinhole until the full repair is done, but we do not leave one as a permanent fix.

We also check the pipe on either side. Pipes that split in one spot are often weak nearby. If corrosion runs through the whole line, we will tell you, and repiping may be a better plan than patching each failure. The same goes for cracked drain pipes. A damaged drain section gets cut out and replaced, and a camera check finds out how far the damage extends.

Signs a pipe has split or is about to

  • A sudden drop in water pressure
  • Water stains spreading on a ceiling or wall
  • The sound of running water when everything is off
  • Frost, a bulge or a bead of water on an exposed pipe
  • A wet baseboard or a puddle with no source
  • A spinning water meter with fixtures closed

Pipe patched but still dripping?

Describe it to dispatch. A small drip can mean a second failure, and we would rather check than have you wait for a flood.

After the repair: drying out and paperwork

Water left in drywall and insulation grows mold within a couple of days. Remove wet carpet, run fans and dehumidifiers, and cut out soaked drywall if it sags. Photograph everything before you clean and keep our written repair summary for your insurer.

Sudden pipe bursts are commonly covered under a homeowner policy, while damage from long-term seepage is often not. Read your policy and call your insurer early. If your meter shows a leak but the source is hidden, our leak detection service can trace it.

Codes and permits

Pipe repairs in Illinois follow the Illinois Plumbing Code. Ask the Village of Romeoville building department whether your repair needs a permit, and keep photos of the damage for your insurer.
