A sewer backup is an emergency at any hour, and our sewer line repair service in Romeoville, IL runs 24/7. Stop using toilets, sinks, showers and the washer as soon as water rises in a floor drain. Everything you flush has nowhere to go, and the lowest fixture in the house will take it.

Sewer backup: what to do before the plumber arrives

  • Stop all water use, including the dishwasher and any running toilet.
  • Keep children and pets out of the wet area. Sewage carries bacteria.
  • Switch off power to a flooded basement only if you can reach the panel dry.
  • Open windows for ventilation and wear rubber gloves if you must touch anything.
  • Take photos for your insurer before cleaning anything up.

How we confirm the sewer line has failed

A backup can come from a clog or from a pipe that has broken. The difference matters, because cleaning a collapsed line only brings relief for a few days. We clear the blockage, then run a camera down the pipe and record what it shows.

The footage tells us where the break is, how far from the house it sits, and what kind of damage it is. You can watch the same video and ask questions. For a planned look without a backup, see sewer inspection.

What damages a sewer line

Tree roots

Fine roots enter at a joint seeking moisture and grow into a mat that catches paper and grease.

Cracks and breaks

Clay tile and cast iron crack with age, ground movement and heavy loads above the pipe.

Belly or sag

Settled soil lets a section dip so waste pools and solids collect.

Offset joints

Pipe ends shift apart and leave a lip that snags debris.

Collapse

A badly deteriorated pipe caves in and blocks the flow almost completely.

Older Romeoville streets and the pipes beneath them

Subdivisions built around the 1950s, like Hampton Park, may still carry original clay or cast iron laterals. Those materials crack and shed scale with age. If your house is that old and has never had its line inspected, a camera look is a cheap way to know what is underground.

Homes near the Des Plaines River or the old canal corridor can also see wetter soil, which loosens joints. We note that on the report so you can compare after the next heavy rain.

Signs the sewer line is damaged

  • Backups return soon after a cleaning
  • Several drains gurgle or back up together
  • A sewer smell in the basement or yard
  • A soggy or sunken patch of lawn over the line
  • Slow drains throughout the house
  • Cracks in a basement slab near a floor drain

A note on septic systems

Most Romeoville homes connect to the village sewer, but some properties nearby use septic tanks. If you have a septic system, a backup can mean a full tank, a clogged outlet or a failed drain field. We trace and repair the pipe between the house and the tank, but we do not pump tanks or repair fields.

Call a septic hauler for pumping, and ask us to check the house line if the problem returns after pumping. If you do not know which kind of system you have, a look at your utility bill or the cleanout location often settles it.

Codes and permits

Sewer repairs follow the Illinois Plumbing Code, and work on the line serving your home often needs a permit and an inspection from the Village of Romeoville building department before the trench is closed.
