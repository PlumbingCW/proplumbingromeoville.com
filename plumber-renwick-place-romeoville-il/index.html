Plumber in Renwick Place, Romeoville, IL

Renwick Place is a Romeoville subdivision that was built out in phases by more than one homebuilder. If you own a home there, your plumbing questions often start with a different one: who is responsible, the builder or a plumber?

This guide covers how to sort that out, how to learn what is behind your walls, and what to watch around the sump and the yard.

Where Renwick Place sits in Romeoville

Patch reported in 2014 that the Village signed an agreement with William Ryan Homes for 167 lots in the former Stone Bluff subdivision, renamed Renwick Place. In 2018 Patch reported that D.R. Horton would finish the remaining 71 lots, with a model home at 2099 W. Helen Dr. near I-55.

Two builders on one project means two sets of paperwork. If you bought resale, the original builder may differ from the one next door. Ask your seller or title company which company built your specific house.

Builder or plumber: who to call first

Many new homes come with a builder warranty of limited length and scope. Terms vary by contract, so read yours before calling anyone. A leak at a fresh joint, a fixture that fails early or a drain that never ran well may be a warranty matter.

Ordinary wear, clogs from use and damage from outside causes usually are not. Report a suspected warranty problem to the builder in writing and keep photos. If water is actively spreading, shut off the supply first and sort out paperwork after.

Finding out what was installed

Your closing folder is the best first stop. Look for the permit card, inspection sign-offs, the water heater manual and any fixture warranty cards. Photos taken before drywall, if the builder offered them, are even better.

  • Look at the pipe material where it enters at the main shutoff.
  • Find the water heater data plate for model and install date.
  • Locate the sump pit, cleanout and any pressure regulator.
  • Ask the Village Building Division what permit records exist for your address.

Nobody should assume what is in your walls. Check it, write it down and keep the notes with your home papers.

Sump pump and grading checks

Groundwater and heavy rain test any basement. A sump pump only helps if water has a place to go. Check that downspouts and the discharge line send water well away from the foundation, and that soil has not settled back toward the house.

A five-minute sump test

Lift the lid and pour in a bucket of water. The pump should start, empty the pit and shut off. If it hums without moving water, stays silent or cycles constantly, call for a sump pump repair visit before the next storm.

Settling and grading are a builder or yard-work topic, not a plumbing fix. Ask about both if puddles collect beside the foundation.

Village facts for Renwick Place homeowners

The Village's 2025 water materials describe water from Village-run groundwater wells, and say Lake Michigan water through the Grand Prairie Water Commission is expected around 2030. The Village says it has reported no lead mains or service lines. Your own fixtures are separate, so test your taps if you are unsure.

The Village's resident packet says spring hydrant flushing can discolor water. Run a cold tap for a few minutes before assuming a problem inside your home. Public Works Utilities handles water questions at 815-886-1870. The Building Division at 1050 W Romeo Rd handles permits at 815-886-7200, and you should call to confirm whether a plumbing job needs one.

Illinois follows the Illinois Plumbing Code, and you should call 811 before any digging. For more on the wider area, see plumbing in Romeoville.

When a leak is hard to find

A hidden leak can show up as a high water bill, a warm patch on a floor or a meter that moves with everything off. Shut off every fixture, then watch the meter. If it still turns, schedule leak detection to trace the source.

Renwick Place plumbing questions

Check your warranty terms first. If the problem looks like a defect and the warranty is active, notify the builder in writing. If water is spreading, stop it first and then make the notice.

Check your closing documents, or ask the title company or Village Building Division. Patch names two builders for the project, so do not assume.

The Village materials report no lead mains or service lines. Interior plumbing and fixtures are separate, so test if you have concerns.

Wet soil and grading that sends water toward the foundation both raise the load. A pump that cycles constantly may also have a stuck float or a failed check valve.

The Village permit page does not name plumbing specifically. Call the Building Division at 815-886-7200 to confirm before work begins.
