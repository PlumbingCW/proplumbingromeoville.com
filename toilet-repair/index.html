Emergency Toilet Repair in Romeoville, IL

A toilet that overflows, leaks at the base or will not stop running can flood a bathroom in minutes. Our toilet repair service in Romeoville, IL is open 24/7, and we also install new toilets when an old one is past saving.

Most toilet trouble starts small. A flapper wears out, the fill valve hisses, a bolt works loose. Then one day the bowl rises, or a puddle forms at the base, and you need help that night. We cover Romeoville 24/7, and the steps below can limit the damage until a plumber arrives.

Toilet running or overflowing: stop the water first

  1. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or turn the small valve on the wall or floor behind the bowl clockwise until it stops.
  2. Do not flush again. A second flush on a blocked bowl sends more water over the rim.
  3. Lay towels around the base and open a window if sewage has reached the floor.
  4. If other fixtures gurgle or the tub is filling, stop using water everywhere and tell dispatch. That points to the main drain, not just the toilet.

Why a toilet keeps running

A running toilet wastes water around the clock and shows up on the next bill. The cause is almost always inside the tank, and it is usually a part that costs little but must be matched to your model.

Leaks at the base and the wax ring

Water around the foot of the toilet is not always a leak from the toilet itself. Check the supply line and shutoff valve first, then wipe the tank dry because condensation can drip down and mimic a leak. If the floor is still wet after that, the wax ring under the bowl has probably failed.

Replacing a wax ring means lifting the toilet, cleaning the flange and setting the bowl back on a fresh seal. We also check the flange for cracks and the subfloor for softness, because rot beneath a long-term leak can be worse than the leak.

Signs the leak has gone beyond the toilet

  • Soft or spongy flooring around the base
  • Toilet rocks when you sit on it
  • Stains on the ceiling in the room below
  • A sewer smell that returns after cleaning
  • Loose or rusted closet bolts

When a clogged toilet needs more than a plunger

One plunge that clears the bowl is fine. A toilet that clogs again within days, or one that backs up the shower, is telling you the problem is farther down. We use a closet auger for bowl-level blockages and refer deeper blockages to drain cleaning or, when the whole house is affected, sewer line cleaning.

Replacing a toilet instead of repairing it

A cracked bowl, a persistent leak from the china itself or repeated repairs on an old tank usually mean replacement is the better path. Modern toilets use less water per flush, and the bolt pattern and rough-in distance, measured from the wall to the center of the flange, decide which models fit your bathroom.

Installation includes removing the old toilet, checking the flange, setting the new bowl on a new seal, connecting a fresh supply line and testing for leaks. Bring the model you picked or ask us what fits the rough-in. We can also advise on taller comfort-height bowls for easier use.

Older Romeoville homes and their original shutoff valves

Subdivisions such as Hampton Park, developed in 1957, hold homes that are many decades old. In houses of that age the valve behind the toilet is often original and has not been turned in years, so it may stick or weep when you try it. Test yours now. If it will not close, ask us to replace it before you need it.
