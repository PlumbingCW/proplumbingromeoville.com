Plumber in Weslake, Romeoville, IL

Weslake is a residential subdivision on the west side of Romeoville, with Taylor Road along its north edge. This page pulls together what Village records say about it and the plumbing checks that make sense for a house here.

It is a guide, not a sales pitch. Use it to decide what you can look at yourself and when a plumber should look at it.

Where Weslake sits in Romeoville

Real estate portals place Weslake on the west side of Romeoville in ZIP 60446, with Taylor Road on its north side and Weber Road to the east. The same listing describes a country club and an association that maintains shared amenities. Those are portal descriptions, so confirm anything about an association with your own paperwork.

Village ordinances give firmer ground. Ordinance 2444-96 rezoned the Weslake Greens development at 1400 to 1500 West Taylor Road from R-3 to R-4, and Ordinance 2573-98 classified territory tied to the Weslake Subdivision as R-4. Zoning is not plumbing, but it tells you which Village rules and which Village office apply when you plan work.

What the Village records do and do not tell you

Village resolutions list final plats for the Weslake Subdivision in stages, by numbered neighborhood and unit. A separate subdivision called Wespark shares the 1400 to 1500 West Taylor Road address range in its plat resolutions. If your paperwork mentions either name, check which one your lot belongs to.

Do not guess pipe material from the plat

A plat approval date does not say what is inside your walls. Look at the supply lines near the water heater and meter, and note the material. A plumber can tell you more from the actual pipe than from any record.

Everyday questions Weslake homeowners bring up

The points below are general plumbing information that holds in any house. A camera inspection of the sewer line makes sense when a lower-level drain backs up more than once, because it shows whether the cause is a clog, roots or a damaged section.

Sediment builds up in many tank heaters over time. When yours rumbles or hot water runs short, water heater service usually starts with a tank flush, then a look at the anode rod, elements or burner.

Village water and utility facts to know

Per the Village water quality report, Romeoville supplies tap water from groundwater, drawn by six deep wells and six shallow ones. The same report lists no lead mains or lead service lines. Your own interior plumbing is separate, so the report does not describe the pipes inside your house.

The Utilities Division within Public Works handles water questions at 815-886-1870. Call it for flushing schedules, low pressure across a whole block or a question about the meter, which belongs to the Village side of the line.

Its permit page does not spell out plumbing permits specifically, so call to confirm what your project needs. Replacing a fixture often differs from moving a line or a water heater.
