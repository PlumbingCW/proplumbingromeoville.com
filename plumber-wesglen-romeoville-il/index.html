Plumber in Wesglen, Romeoville, IL

Wesglen is a residential subdivision in Romeoville that the Village zoned for R-5 and R-6 housing. Listings describe a mix of single-family houses and townhomes, and the two types raise different plumbing questions.

This guide covers what to check first, when a problem is worth a call, and which Village contacts can answer questions a plumber cannot.

Where Wesglen sits in Romeoville

Real estate guides place Wesglen in southwest Romeoville. Wesglen Park, at 120 S. Wesglen Pkwy., is a Village park, and the Village approved a design phase for renovating its playground in 2023. If you are not sure whether a problem is inside your property line, a park or street next door is a useful landmark when you describe the location to anyone you call.

Townhome or single-family: what changes

Listings for Wesglen show both housing types. The plumbing basics are the same, but the responsibilities and risks differ, and that is general information, not a statement about any one building.

  • In a house, the sewer lateral and water service line from the street to your walls are normally your responsibility.
  • In a townhome, a drain stack or supply riser may serve more than one unit, so a leak or backup can start next door.
  • Shared-wall homes carry sound and water travel between units, so a stain on a shared wall deserves a quick call to your neighbor and your association.
  • Houses with a basement have floor drains and a sump to check. Units without one still have a water heater and washer supply to inspect.

Know your shutoffs

Find the main water shutoff and each fixture valve before you need them. In a townhome, ask whether your unit has its own shutoff or shares one with neighbors. Exercise each valve once a year so it turns when it matters.

What the Village says about water

The Village of Romeoville runs its own groundwater wells, six deep and six shallow, according to its 2025 Water Quality Report. Lake Michigan water through the Grand Prairie Water Commission is planned for around 2030, not today. Groundwater is typically hard, so scale on fixtures and heaters is worth watching.

The Village reports no lead mains or service lines. Public Works Utilities Division handles supply questions at 815-886-1870. The Resident Packet also notes that spring hydrant flushing can briefly discolor water, so a cloudy tap in that season is not automatically a plumbing fault.

When a sewer question needs a camera

Repeated backups, or a drain that clears and then returns, can come from the line beyond the fixture. A sewer camera inspection shows the pipe condition instead of guessing, which matters before you pay for a repair. Homeowners in a townhome row should also ask which section of line is theirs.

Not sure where the problem starts?

Describe the symptom and which fixtures are affected. That is usually enough to decide whether it is a quick fix or needs a visit.

Permits, codes and digging

Plumbing work in Illinois follows the Illinois Plumbing Code. The Village Building Division, at 1050 W Romeo Rd, handles permits, and its permit page does not mention plumbing permits specifically, so call 815-886-7200 to confirm before work starts. Before any digging, such as a fence post or a new line, call 811 so utilities can be marked. Learn more about plumbing in Romeoville.
