One dispatch point, six communities

Romeoville Pro Plumbing answers an emergency line 24/7 and sends a licensed plumber from 402 N Weber Rd. Romeoville homes use ZIP 60446. The five towns on this page are the ones we reach most often, and each has its own page with local detail.

Which direction we travel

Bolingbrook lies north and northeast of the office. Plainfield is to the west. Lockport is south and southwest, Joliet is southwest, and Crest Hill is south. Tell dispatch your street and cross street, and we will give you the plan for your address.

Drive times depend on traffic and the time of day, so we do not promise an arrival window. We do tell you when the truck is on its way.

What changes from town to town

Bolingbrook grew from early 1960s tract subdivisions, so some blocks have fifty-year-old drain lines worth a camera look. Lockport has a canal-era downtown with some of the oldest buildings in the area, where original pipe is a real possibility.

Plainfield uses Lake Michigan water and has grown fast since the 1990s, so newer homes dominate and water heaters are a common call. Joliet is the county seat with large historic districts, which means older drain stacks and clay laterals. Crest Hill runs its own wells and bars rainwater from the sanitary sewer, which is why a sump pump discharge matters there.

None of this changes the process. It changes what the technician brings and what questions dispatch asks.

Why local service matters in an emergency

A burst pipe or a sewer backup does not wait for a technician coming from across the county. Starting from Weber Road keeps the truck close to I-55 and Route 53, which are the roads most of these towns share.

Every town has its own water supply, sewer system and housing age. A rooted clay lateral in an older neighborhood is a different job from a sump pump failure in a newer subdivision. Our town pages cover those differences so you know what to expect.

Services available in every town

What to tell dispatch from any town

  1. Your street address and the nearest cross street.
  2. What is leaking, backing up or not working, and since when.
  3. Whether water is still moving and whether the main valve is off.
  4. A phone number that stays on, plus who can approve the repair.

With those four items, dispatch can match a technician and tools to the job before the truck leaves Weber Road. It also helps when a caller is stressed and details are slipping.

Renters, landlords and businesses

Every one of these towns has rental houses, apartments and small shops. We take calls from tenants, landlords and property managers. If a tenant calls, we confirm with the owner before starting anything beyond making the problem safe.

Commercial customers can read about commercial plumbing for after-hours service at shops, warehouses and offices.

If your town is not listed, call anyway. We may still reach you, and dispatch will tell you plainly whether we can send a plumber to your address tonight.

Homeowners in neighboring villages often ask about the same services. Burst pipes, backed-up floor drains and failed water heaters make up most late-night calls across the whole area, so the emergency process is identical everywhere.

Each town gets the full list. The most requested are drain cleaning, water heater replacement, sewer line repair and leak detection. Browse the full services list if your problem is not here.
