Plumber in Lakewood Falls, Romeoville, IL

Lakewood Falls is a name that crosses a municipal edge. Some homes with this name sit inside the Village of Romeoville and others carry a Plainfield or Bolingbrook label in school and listing sites. This page covers the Romeoville part only.

Before you call anyone about water, sewer or permits, confirm which government serves your address. That one check decides who answers your utility questions and who reviews your work.

Where the name Lakewood Falls applies

Village ordinance 2647-99, dated April 5, 1999, classified newly annexed land described as Lakewood Homes/Lakewood Falls Phase 5. It set the residential parcel as R-6 and a commercial parcel as B-2. That is a Village record, so at least that phase is part of Romeoville.

A real estate profile describes the community as south of Lockport Road and west of Weber Road, with single-family homes, condos and townhouses. Those are portal descriptions, not Village boundaries. Treat them as a rough guide and verify your own lot.

Check your address before you rely on this page

School listings and some real estate sites file the name under Plainfield or Bolingbrook. A different municipality can mean a different water utility, different permit office and different rules. Your water bill and the Village Public Works Utilities Division can settle it in one call.

What the Village says about water and who to call

The Village runs its own groundwater wells, 6 deep and 6 shallow, according to its 2025 Water Quality Report. Lake Michigan water through the Grand Prairie Water Commission is planned around 2030, not in place today. The report lists no lead mains or lead service lines.

Tie-back: for water quality, billing or a suspected main issue, call the Public Works Utilities Division at 815-886-1870. Mention your street so staff can tell if your address is in the Village service area.

The Resident Packet says spring hydrant flushing can discolor water. If water turns cloudy or tinted after crews are in the area, run a cold tap for a few minutes before blaming your own pipes or hot water tank.

The Village water service area includes parts of unincorporated Plainfield and Bolingbrook. That is why a Village water bill does not prove your home is inside Romeoville.

Permits and digging

The Building Division at 1050 W Romeo Rd handles permits and can be reached at 815-886-7200. Its permit page does not mention plumbing permits by name, so call to confirm what your project needs. Ask especially about a water heater swap, a new drain line or work on a shared wall.

Illinois Plumbing Code governs the work. Before any digging for a line repair or sump discharge, call 811 so utilities can mark their lines. Marking helps avoid hits on buried gas, electric and water lines.

If a basement drain backs up or you suspect a cracked line, a camera look beforehand saves guesswork. Our sewer inspection page explains what a camera shows. For larger plumbing in Romeoville see plumbing in Romeoville.

Lakewood Falls plumbing questions

Part of it is. Village ordinance 2647-99 classified land called Lakewood Homes/Lakewood Falls Phase 5 when it was annexed in 1999. Real estate sites also place the community south of Lockport Road and west of Weber Road.

Other sites label the name as Plainfield or Bolingbrook, so check your own address with the Village.

Check who sends your water bill. If the Village of Romeoville does, call the Public Works Utilities Division with your address and ask.

The Village water service area also reaches parts of unincorporated Plainfield and Bolingbrook, so a Village water bill alone does not prove Village limits.

The Village's 2025 water quality report says it has no lead mains or lead service lines on record. Pipes and fixtures inside a house are a separate matter and belong to the owner.

If you want to know what is inside your own walls, a plumber can look at the pipe material at the meter and under sinks.

The Village plans to receive Lake Michigan water through the Grand Prairie Water Commission around 2030. Today the supply is Village groundwater wells.

Plans can move, so ask the Utilities Division for current timing before you buy treatment equipment based on it.

The Village Building Division handles permits, but its permit page does not mention plumbing specifically. Call and ask before work that changes pipes, drains or a water heater.

Illinois Plumbing Code applies either way, and a plumber should work to it.
