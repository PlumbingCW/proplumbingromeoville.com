Pump not working and the basement is taking on water

Check the simple things first. Confirm the pump is plugged in and that the outlet has power, since a tripped GFCI is a common cause. Lift the float with a stick. If the pump starts, the float is stuck or tangled and will need adjusting or replacing.

If the pump stays silent after that, or hums without pushing water, shut off its power and call. Running a seized motor can burn it out and make the repair bigger.

Why a pump fails when you need it most

Pumps sit idle for months and then run hard for hours. Silt and gravel collect in the pit. Floats stick against the basin wall. A rubber check valve loses its seal. Any of these can stop a pump on the first heavy rain after a dry spell.

Power loss is the other big cause. Summer storms often take out electricity just as the pit fills, and a plug-in pump cannot run without it. A battery backup or a water-powered unit covers that gap.

Warning signs before it fails

  • Pump runs far longer than it used to after rain
  • Rust streaks on the housing or a rotten egg smell from the pit
  • No test cycle: lift the float or pour in a bucket of water and the motor does not start
  • The pump is older than the basement finishing and was never serviced
  • Water shows in the pit after dry weather and the pump never rests

Repair or replace the pump

A new switch or check valve is a small repair. A motor that has run dry or seized is usually cheaper to replace than to rebuild. We tell you which one fits, and what a larger pump or a second pump would add if your pit takes in a lot of water.

Battery backup and a second pump

A backup system adds a battery-powered pump beside the main one. If power fails or the main pump jams, the backup takes over and keeps the pit from overflowing. Batteries need testing and replacing on a schedule, so a backup that was installed and forgotten may not run when called.

Sump pit and ejector pump: not the same job

A sump pump moves groundwater and rainwater away from the foundation. An ejector pump lifts wastewater from a basement bathroom, laundry or floor drain up to the sewer line. We service both, but they run on different lines and different rules, so tell dispatch which one you have.

If the pit smells like sewage or a toilet gurgles when the pump runs, the water in the pit may not be groundwater at all. That points to an ejector or a sewer backup, and our sewer line cleaning crew can clear the main.

Low ground near the river

Homes close to the Des Plaines River and the Illinois and Michigan Canal often sit on flat, low ground, where groundwater can rise after a long wet spell. For these houses the pump runs more often, and the discharge line matters. It should empty well away from the foundation, not onto a slope that drains back toward the house.
