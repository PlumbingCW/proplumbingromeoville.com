Emergency Water Heater Repair in Romeoville, IL

A water heater that leaks, trips a breaker or stops making hot water will not wait for business hours. Our licensed plumbers repair gas and electric tank units in Romeoville day or night, and tell you plainly when a repair is not worth it.

Our emergency water heater repair service in Romeoville, IL runs 24/7. A cold shower is an annoyance. A tank that is pouring water across a basement floor is a different problem, and it needs a plumber the same day. Most calls start with one of three things: a leak, no hot water at all, or hot water that runs out in minutes.

If water is leaking from the heater right now

  1. Close the cold water valve on the pipe feeding the top of the tank. A quarter turn handle is parallel to the pipe when open and crosswise when shut.
  2. Switch off power. Flip the breaker for an electric unit, or turn the gas control to pilot or off on a gas unit.
  3. Do not touch the tank or the floor around it if water has reached an outlet or cord.
  4. Move boxes and stored items out of the puddle's path, and tell dispatch where the water is coming from.

Where the water appears matters. A drip from the pipe fittings on top is usually a cheap repair. Water seeping from the base of the tank itself means the steel shell has rusted through, and no repair will fix that. Our replacement crew handles those jobs the same day when stock allows.

Hot water gone: gas and electric fault paths

No hot water splits into two very different checks. On a gas unit we look at the pilot, the thermocouple, the gas control valve and the flue draft. A pilot that lights and then drops out usually points at a worn thermocouple. A pilot that will not light at all points at the gas control or the supply.

On an electric unit the culprits are the upper or lower heating element, the thermostats and the high limit reset button. A tank that makes lukewarm water often has a dead lower element. A tank with no hot water at all often has a tripped limit switch or a failed upper element.

Signs your water heater needs a plumber

  • Rumbling, popping or knocking from the tank as it heats
  • Rust-colored hot water, or a metallic smell
  • Hot water that runs out far sooner than it used to
  • Water pooled at the base of the unit
  • A gas smell near the heater, which means leave and call the gas utility first

Why a loud tank is usually a full tank

Sediment settles on the bottom of every tank. The burner or lower element then heats the sediment layer rather than the water. Trapped steam bubbles pop through it, which is the knocking you hear. Flushing helps early on. In an old tank the crust can be thick enough that flushing only stirs up the mess.

Mineral content in the supply decides how fast this happens. Ask the Village of Romeoville for its latest water quality report, or test your tap with a hardness strip. Harder water means more scale on elements and tank floors, and a shorter life for both.

Repair or replace: how we weigh it

A thermostat, element, thermocouple or relief valve is a sensible repair on a tank in decent shape. A tank that is leaking from the shell is finished. Between those cases we look at age, rust at the fittings, and the cost of the part against a new unit. If the tank has outlived its useful years, we say so before any parts are bought.

If you are leaning toward a new unit, our water heater installation page explains sizing, venting and expansion tank choices. Homes that want endless hot water can look at a tankless model instead of another tank.
