What counts as a plumbing emergency in Bolingbrook

Call the emergency line now if you see any of these

  • Water spreading across a floor from a pipe, heater or supply line
  • Sewage or gray water rising from a basement floor drain
  • No hot water at all in a house with young children or elderly residents
  • A pipe that has split and cannot be isolated at a fixture valve
  • A gas smell near a water heater (leave the house and call the gas utility first)

Everything else, such as a dripping faucet or a slow tub, can usually wait for a daytime visit. If you are unsure, call anyway. Dispatch will tell you whether it is safe to wait.

Lake Michigan water from Illinois American Water

Illinois American Water, a private utility, supplies drinking water in Bolingbrook, and news coverage describes the supply as Lake Michigan water. The village does not run the system. If the pressure drops or the water turns cloudy on your whole street, the utility handles the main. Your own plumbing starts at the meter.

We could not find a hardness number in an official report for the village, so we will not guess one. Your utility's annual water report lists it. A quick tap test from a hardware store also tells you whether scale on your heater element is likely, and whether a water softener is worth pricing.

Homes from the 1960s and the pipes inside them

According to the village's own history page, the first tracts went up in the early 1960s. Westbury, Colonial Village and King's Park were built by Dover Construction Company and sold between 1961 and 1965. Homes that age are now past the point where original drain lines, shutoff valves and water heaters are in their best years.

Population jumped from about 7,600 in 1970 to more than 37,000 in 1980 as the village annexed land, so many homes date from the 1970s and 1980s too. If a valve under a sink will not turn or a heater tank is rusting at the seams, a planned water heater replacement costs less than a flooded basement.

Sewers, storm drains and heavy rain

The village Public Works Department runs three water reclamation facilities and maintains the storm sewers and catch basins in the street. The pipe from your house to the village main is yours. When rain overwhelms the street system, water can find your basement through that private pipe or a failed sump pump. Local news reported about 75 homes damaged in the April 2013 flooding.

If you see a blocked storm drain on your block, report it to the village. If water is coming up inside, call us for sewer line cleaning or a sump pump repair, and we will work with you on the emergency fix.

Before we arrive

Find the main shutoff

It is usually near the meter or where the line enters the basement. Turn it clockwise until the flow stops.

Give the cross streets

Name the closest major road, such as Boughton, Route 53, Veterans Parkway or Weber, along with your address.

Clear a path

Move boxes from the basement stairs and the area around the heater or drain so the plumber can start work at once.

Photograph the damage

Take a few pictures before cleanup. Your insurer may ask for them later.

Keep a pet-safe room

Hold dogs and cats in another room. Tools, hot water and open drains are hazardous for them.

Permits and licensing in Bolingbrook

The village Building Division requires permits for repairs as well as new work, and it lists the current Illinois State Plumbing Code among the codes it enforces. Inspections need three business days of notice. In an emergency we stop the damage first, then explain whether the permanent repair needs a permit. Illinois licenses plumbers through the state health department, and you can look up a license on its site.
